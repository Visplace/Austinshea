Introduction

Where Contracts, Operations, and Financial Performance Meet

My experience spans lease administration, mixed-use property operations, financial reporting, and property-level analysis. What sets the work apart is resolving the points where lease language, billing systems, operating practices, and financial results do not line up.

In practice, that means tracing a charge back through the lease and its amendments — rent, CAM, real estate taxes, insurance, percentage rent, abatements, expense caps, and recoveries — and confirming that billing, reconciliations, and reporting reflect what the agreement actually requires.

I bring an analytical, finance-oriented view to it: organizing complex information, documenting conclusions clearly, and coordinating across accounting, legal, ownership, leasing, vendors, and property teams.

Method

How I Approach the Work

1

Read the Source Documents

Financial and operating conclusions should trace back to the governing agreement, amendment, supporting record, or verified assumption.

2

Connect Language to Cash Flow

A lease provision is not just contractual language. It can directly affect revenue, recoveries, expenses, timing, risk, and asset value.

3

Document the Reasoning

Strong analysis should allow another stakeholder to understand the issue, the supporting information, and the conclusion reached.

4

Solve the Underlying Problem

The goal is not simply to identify a discrepancy. It is to determine what happened, what the agreement requires, and what action should follow.
